**Date:** 10/05/2026

**Working game title:** Yakin

**Project stage:** Early concept and prototyping

**Entry type:** Concept development and design revision

## Concept and Decision

**Current concept and intended experience:**

Yakin is a social deduction game set during the night shift of a Japanese konbini. One player is the employee and the other players are customers, but one customer is secretly a yokai. The customers have to observe each other, communicate, and identify the yokai before it eliminates them. The employee is the only player who has the ability to defeat the yokai, so the customers need to give the employee the correct information in time.

I want the game to create suspicion and tension between the players while still having a funny and casual style. Players should constantly question whether someone is acting strangely because they are the yokai or because they are simply another suspicious customer.

**Design question or decision:**

The main question is how the yokai can secretly eliminate customers without immediately revealing who they are. I also need to determine how customers collect enough information to make logical deductions without making the yokai too easy to identify.

**Selected concept and alternatives kept:**

I selected Yakin as my main concept because the hidden-role gameplay gives players more opportunities to interact with each other. I am keeping **Onigiri Dash** as an alternative idea for a future digital game.

**Peer feedback that affected the decision, if received (otherwise say unavailable):**

Feedback from JL changed the direction of the game. My original concept focused more on the employee dealing with strange customers and trying to determine which ones were supernatural.

The feedback suggested putting the employee and customers together inside the konbini and making one customer secretly the yokai. The yokai would try to eliminate the customers and eventually the employee. The customers would have to identify the yokai and warn the employee, while the employee would have the power needed to defeat it.

I decided to explore this version because it gives every player a clearer purpose and creates more interaction between the employee, customers, and hidden yokai.

## Reading Connection

Choose one idea from Chapter 1: Having the Idea, The Treatment, or Feasibility.

**Section and specific idea:**

Feasibility — determining whether an idea can realistically be developed with the available time, skills, tools, and project scope.

**Relevance today:** Still relevant

**Reason and supporting example:**

Feasibility is important because the updated version of Yakin could become complicated very quickly. I could add many different yokai, special abilities, items, rooms in the konbini, and different ways to eliminate players. However, adding all of those systems before testing the basic idea would make the project harder to develop and would not prove that the main gameplay is actually fun.

**Connection to a choice, question or proposed test for my game:**

I decided that the first prototype should only include one employee, one yokai, several customers, and a simple way to gather clues and eliminate players. This will allow me to test the social deduction mechanic before adding different yokai or more complicated abilities.

## Progress and Evidence

**What I created, changed, tested, or decided:**

I selected Yakin as my main game concept and revised its gameplay after receiving feedback. The game changed from mainly having an employee investigate strange customers to having multiple players together inside the konbini with one hidden yokai among them.

I also established three basic player roles: the employee, normal customers, and the hidden yokai. The customers help identify the threat, the employee has the ability to defeat it, and the yokai tries to eliminate players while remaining hidden.

**Direct artifact link or specific observation:**

My concept pitch, peer feedback, selection sheet, and one-page treatment document the development of the idea. A specific observation from the feedback was that giving the customers an active role in identifying the yokai creates more interaction than having the employee make all of the important decisions.

**What the evidence confirms:**

The brainstorming and feedback confirm that the konbini setting can support a multiplayer social deduction game and that each player can have a different objective while still contributing to the same central conflict.

**What remains uncertain:**

The biggest unanswered question is how the yokai secretly eliminates customers. I also need to decide what clues players receive, how much information they can share, how the employee uses their power, and what happens if the employee attacks the wrong player.
