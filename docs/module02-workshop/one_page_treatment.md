# IT265 Module 2: One-Page Treatment

**Working game title:** Yakin

## Four-Sentence Core

1. **Premise:** In Yakin, an employee and several customers are inside a Japanese konbini during the night shift, but one of the customers is secretly a yokai hiding among the group.

2. **Experience and loop:** Players observe each other, communicate, gather clues, and decide who they can trust while the hidden yokai tries to act like a normal customer and secretly eliminate the others.

3. **Goal and pressure:** The customers must identify the yokai and warn the employee in time so the employee can defeat it, while the yokai tries to eliminate the customers and ultimately reach the employee to win.

4. **Scope:** The first version focuses only on the hidden-role, deduction, and communication mechanics and can be tested using role cards, action or clue cards, tokens, and a simple paper representation of the konbini.

## Treatment Draft

**Yakin** is a casual social deduction game set during the night shift of a Japanese konbini. One player takes the role of the employee while the other players are customers inside the store. However, one of the customers is secretly a yokai pretending to be human. The players know that something supernatural is hiding among them, but they do not know which customer it is.

During the game, the customers observe each other, communicate, and gather information to determine who is behaving suspiciously. The yokai participates in these interactions while trying to appear like a normal customer. Its goal is to create confusion, avoid suspicion, and secretly eliminate the other customers. This creates a repeated cycle where players gather clues, discuss what they have seen, decide who they trust, and try to identify the hidden threat.

The employee has a different role from the customers. The employee is the only player with the ability to defeat the yokai, but they need the other players to help determine who it is. The customers therefore need to communicate their suspicions and warn the employee before too many of them are eliminated. Incorrect accusations can give the yokai more time and create distrust within the group.

The pressure increases as customers are eliminated and fewer players remain to help identify the yokai. The human side wins if the customers correctly identify the hidden yokai and the employee successfully defeats it. The yokai wins if it avoids discovery long enough to eliminate the other customers and eventually reach the employee.

What makes Yakin distinctive is the combination of social deduction with an ordinary Japanese convenience store that becomes increasingly dangerous during the night shift. The atmosphere can mix humor and Japanese supernatural folklore with light horror without becoming a serious horror game.

The first prototype will use simple physical materials such as employee, customer, and yokai role cards, clue or action cards, and tokens. The first test will focus on whether the yokai can hide its actions while still leaving enough information for the other players to make meaningful deductions.
