#### Progress
- **What I accomplished**:
  > During this module, I learned more about what makes something a game and how gameplay is created through the interaction between the player, rules, goals, challenges and their mechanics. I also started working on the pitch for my game, We started brainstorming couple ideas and concepts. So far I have develped two main concepts that I could potencially pitch. The first is "Yakin" a game based on working the night shift at a Japanese Konbini where unusual customers, including yokai, ghosts, delinquents or drunk customers, can enter the store. I also explored a multiplayer version where one player is the employee while the other players pretend to be normal customers while secretly being supernatural creatures.
  > My second concept is onigiri Dash, a casual endless-runner style game but since it has to be played with 2 or more people I'm thinking of making it like Krash instead. more like a race game.
- **Challenges faced**:
  >  One challenge was coming up with an idea that was creative enough but could still have a clear gameplay and not overly complicated to develop. It was easy to think of funny themes or characters, but I also had to think about what the player would actually do, what the objective would be, and what would make the game entertaining. I also had several possible directions for Yakin, so trying to fir the multiplayer version was a challenge aswell.
- **Solutions**:
  > Instead of trying to decide every detail immediately, I tried to have different concepts and see what are the different possibilities for mechanics and other features. For Yakin, I considered both a card based version and a multiplayer version. I'm also still trying to figure out the entire game flow and what features as well as character would it be. And how would I make the physical demo for it.

#### Learnings
> One of the main things I learned in this module is that having an interesting idea or theme is only the beginning of designing a game. I also need to think about the gameplay itself, including the player's goals, the rules, challenges, actions, and how all those systems interact. I learn that brainstorming and crating an initial concept does not mean every details has to be decided immediately. An idea can change as the mechanics are explored, tested and improved.
