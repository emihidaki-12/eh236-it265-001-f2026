# IT265 Module 3 Dev Log

## Name: Emi Hidaki

### Module: Module 003 - Game Balance

### Date: 10/05/2026

#### Goals for this Module

- [x] Understand the basic concepts of game balance
- [x] Learn why balance is important for creating a fair and enjoyable player experience
- [x] Explore how different player roles, abilities, and rules affect balance
- [x] Review case studies and evaluate balance decisions in existing games
- [x] Apply game balance concepts to my current game idea, Yakin
- [ ] Determine the exact abilities and limitations for each role in Yakin

#### Progress

- **What I accomplished**:
  - Reviewed the Game Balance material and learned how different mechanics, player abilities, rewards, and challenges can affect the overall experience of a game.
  - Reviewed the Case Study Evaluation material and looked at how game mechanics can be evaluated instead of only deciding whether a game is fun or not.
  - Continued developing my game concept, **Yakin**, and started thinking about the balance between the employee, customers, and hidden yokai.
  - Identified some important balance questions that will need to be tested during the prototype.

- **Challenges faced**:
  - One of the biggest challenges is making sure that the yokai is powerful enough to create tension without making the game unfair for the other players.
  - Since the employee is the only player who can defeat the yokai, the employee could become too powerful if identifying and attacking the yokai is too easy.
  - On the other hand, if the customers do not receive enough clues, finding the yokai could become random guessing instead of actual deduction.
  - I also still need to decide how the yokai can eliminate customers without immediately revealing its identity.

- **Solutions**:
  - I plan to start with simple abilities for each role instead of adding too many mechanics at once.
  - The customers should have ways to gather information and communicate their suspicions, but they should not receive enough information to immediately identify the yokai.
  - The employee's ability to defeat the yokai should have a limitation or consequence so that the employee cannot randomly attack every customer.
  - These mechanics can be adjusted after testing the physical prototype and seeing which side has an advantage.

#### Learnings

- Game balance does not necessarily mean that every player has the exact same abilities. Different roles can have different strengths and weaknesses while still creating a fair overall experience.
- Small changes to rules, abilities, information, and consequences can have a large effect on how players make decisions.
- Evaluating a game requires looking at how its mechanics affect player behavior and whether the intended experience is actually happening.
- Playtesting is important because a mechanic that sounds balanced on paper may work very differently when real players interact with it.
- For **Yakin**, balance will be especially important because the employee, customers, and yokai have different information, abilities, and objectives.

#### Free Thinking

- For **Yakin**, I want each role to feel important without making one role too powerful.
- The yokai could have the ability to secretly eliminate customers, but there should be some type of clue or evidence left behind when this happens.
- Customers could have different ways to investigate or observe other players and then communicate what they discover to the employee.
- The employee could have a powerful ability that can defeat the yokai, but using it on an innocent customer should have a consequence.
- One possibility is limiting how many times the employee can use their ability. This would make choosing who to trust more important.
- I also want to experiment with how many customers are needed for the game to feel balanced. A game with one yokai and three customers may feel very different from one yokai and six customers.

#### Next Steps

- Create the first simple physical prototype of **Yakin**.
- Define the basic abilities for the employee, customers, and yokai.
- Decide how the yokai can secretly eliminate another player.
- Create a basic clue or information system for the customers.
- Decide what happens if the employee attacks the wrong person.
- Playtest the game and observe whether the employee/customer team or the yokai has an unfair advantage.
- Adjust the rules and abilities based on the results of the first playtest.
