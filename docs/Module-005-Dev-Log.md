# IT265 Module 005 Dev Log

## Name: Emi Hidaki

### Module: Module 005 - Game Mechanics, Decks, Boards and Movement

### Date: 10/19/2026

#### Goals for this Module

- [x] Understand how game mechanics affect player decisions and interactions.
- [x] Learn how decks and cards can be used to create different gameplay situations.
- [x] Explore how boards and movement influence the player's experience.
- [x] Review the Atari visual redesign case study requirements.
- [x] Think about how these concepts could be applied to my game, Yakin.
- [ ] Decide on the final movement and card mechanics for Yakin.
- [ ] Create and test a simple physical prototype.

#### Progress

- **What I accomplished**:

  - Reviewed the Game Mechanics lesson and explored how rules, actions, and player decisions work together to create gameplay.
  - Studied how decks and cards can introduce different actions, events, and information into a game.
  - Learned about boards and movement and how the layout of a game can affect the decisions players make.
  - Continued developing ideas for Yakin, especially how the customers, employee, and hidden yokai would interact inside the konbini.
  - Reviewed the Atari case study assignment, which focuses on redesigning the visual appearance of an existing game while keeping its original mechanics.

- **Challenges faced**:

  - One of my biggest challenges is deciding how movement will work in Yakin. I want players to explore different areas of the konbini, but I do not want the movement system to become too complicated.
  - Another challenge is figuring out how the yokai can secretly eliminate customers without immediately revealing its identity.
  - I also need to make sure that any cards or special actions are balanced so that one role does not have too much of an advantage.

- **Solutions**:

  - I am considering starting with a simple board representing the konbini, with areas such as the cashier, shelves, storage room, and entrance.
  - Players could move between these areas during their turns and interact with other players or objects.
  - I could use cards to represent different actions, clues, or events. This could make the game more interesting without requiring too many complicated rules.
  - I want to start with only a few basic mechanics and test them before adding more features.

#### Learnings

- Game mechanics are important because they determine what players can do and how they interact with the game.
- Decks and cards can be used for more than random events. They can also provide information, limit actions, and create strategic decisions.
- The layout of a board can influence player movement, interaction, and strategy.
- Movement should have a purpose instead of simply allowing players to travel between locations.
- I also learned that visual design can improve how players understand and experience a game without changing its original rules.
- For Yakin, I think combining simple movement with hidden roles and action cards could create interesting situations between the players.

#### Free Thinking

- I have been thinking about making the konbini itself an important part of Yakin's gameplay.
- Instead of having everyone stay in one place, players could move around the store to complete small tasks, investigate suspicious activity, or interact with other customers.
- The yokai could use certain areas of the store to secretly attack another player. However, there should be clues that help the remaining players figure out what happened.
- I also like the idea of using different types of cards. For example, investigation cards could help customers discover information, while action cards could allow players to perform special actions.
- The employee could have a limited ability to defeat the yokai, which would make communication between customers and the employee more important.
- I want the game to feel suspenseful but still casual and fun. I think keeping the board small and the actions simple would help with that.

#### Next Steps

- Sketch a simple layout of the konbini for the first prototype.
- Decide how many spaces players can move during each turn.
- Create basic role cards for the employee, customers, and yokai.
- Experiment with investigation and action cards.
- Decide how the yokai can eliminate players while remaining hidden.
- Test whether movement and cards make the game more interesting or unnecessarily complicated.
- Continue working on the Atari visual redesign case study and its required worksheet.
- Use feedback and playtesting to improve the mechanics before adding more features.
