#### Learnings
- I learned that colors are not just used to make a game look nice, but they can also communicate information to players.
- Warm colors like red, orange, and yellow can create feelings of energy or warmth, while cool colors like blue and purple can create a calmer or more mysterious atmosphere.
- I also learned about color harmony and how different combinations can make a game's visual design feel more balanced.
- Contrast is important because it helps players identify characters, objects, and possible dangers.
- From my case study, I realized that even older games with limited graphics could still use colors and character designs to communicate information.

#### Free Thinking
- For our convenience store game, I was thinking about using bright and warm colors for the normal store environment to make it feel like a real Japanese konbini.
- Since one of the customers is secretly a yokai, I think it would be interesting if the colors or lighting changed when something suspicious happens.
- For example, the store could normally have bright white and yellow lighting, but during dangerous moments, the environment could become darker with blue, purple, or red tones.
- I also think we should make the employee, customers, and impostor visually recognizable without making the impostor too obvious.
- This could help create suspense because players would have to pay attention to small details to figure out who the impostor is.
